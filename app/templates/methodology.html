The due date is the discovery date plus the target. An open finding past its due date is overdue. Analysts can override a due date, and the change is recorded in the finding's history.

This score ranks work. It doesn't measure real-world likelihood or business impact, and it uses CVSS base scores without temporal or environmental adjustment.

STIG severity categories (illustrative)

DISA Security Technical Implementation Guides rate each check as CAT I, II or III. Each STIG rule references Control Correlation Identifiers (CCIs), which map back to SP 800-53 controls. That chain is how a failed configuration check becomes evidence against a control. SecureScope models the last link: each finding names the controls it is evidence against.

Control verification states

  • Verified: assessed as satisfied within the last {{ valid_days }} days.
  • Implemented, not verified: reported as implemented or partial, with no assessment yet.
  • Assessment expired: the last satisfied assessment is more than {{ valid_days }} days old.
  • Deficient: assessed and found other than satisfied.
  • Not implemented: planned or not implemented.
  • Contradicted by findings: verified, but open high or critical findings reference the control.
